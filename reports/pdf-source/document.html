Metalix UI/UX System
Foundations

The system on one page

Every screen answers one of the owner's two daily questions: "What do I need to buy?" and "What can I deliver?". The rest of this guide is these eight principles applied to each part of the app.

1

Numbers first, calm around them

Neutral greys plus one steel-blue accent. Colour appears only when something needs attention (amber = close to a limit, red = over a limit). Big, aligned, exact numbers.

2

One way to do each thing

One sidebar, one page layout, one table (that becomes cards on phones), one form pattern, one number formatter. Learn it once, use it everywhere.

3

Prevent errors, don't report them

The delivery form shows PO remaining, yard stock and supplier stock before saving, names the tightest limit and offers "Use max". Mistakes are hard to make.

4

Three clicks per core task

Smart defaults (today, last supplier, last rate), "Deliver" buttons right on the order row, inline "+ Add" in pickers, "Save & add another".

5

Plain trade words

"Left to deliver", "Stock with supplier", "Rate per ton". Sales POs are "Sales Orders"; statuses are Pending, Partly delivered, Completed, Cancelled.

6

Never a blank screen

Old rows stay visible while new data loads; skeletons only on the first load and only after 0.5 s; forms keep what users typed through every error.

7

Motion only as feedback

100–300 ms, never more than 400 ms. Animations confirm a press, a save or a change. No count-ups, shakes, confetti or parallax.

8

Built for the yard too

44–48 px touch targets, bottom navigation, drawers instead of dialogs, cards instead of tables, and a high-contrast "Sunlight" theme for outdoors.

Rules

  • Every item has an icon and a text label, never an icon alone.

Foundations

Page anatomy, containers and shortcuts

Every page is built the same way, and every create/edit form opens in the same kind of container. Users stop hunting for buttons after the first day.

One page anatomy

Padding 24 px (16 px on phones). Lists use the full width; forms stay ≈ 560–640 px wide; detail pages split 2/3 + 1/3 from 1024 px.

Which container for which job

  • Sheets keep the list visible behind them, so users never lose their place (NN/g: modals make people forget details they need).
  • Sheets have URLs (/sales/new?poId=0142): refreshing never loses the form, and drafts are saved locally.
  • On phones every Sheet and Dialog becomes a bottom Drawer.

Dashboard rules and data visualisation

A dashboard is the most important information "arranged on a single screen so it can be monitored at a glance" (Stephen Few). Every number needs context to show whether it is good or bad, and every number should lead somewhere.

Pick the chart by the question

QuestionChartDesign rule
Do I have enough of each material for open orders?Horizontal grouped barsSorted by shortage; end labels "Short 24.500 t"; axis starts at 0
How far is this order delivered?Progress / bullet bar"18.250 of 30.000 t · 61%"
Are we buying faster than we deliver?Two-line chartDirect labels at line ends; solid vs dashed
Are buy and sell rates moving? (later)Rate trend lineNeeds a per-material rates endpoint first
Exact values for many materialsTable with inline barsSwitch automatically when there are more than 8 materials

Do

  • Label data directly on bars and line ends
  • Use colour only to flag attention (amber/red) plus an icon and words
  • Give every number a comparison: "▲ 8% vs Aug"
  • Make every card, row and bar a link to its filtered list
  • On phones print values on the bars; tooltips don't work on touch

Don't

  • Pie, donut, gauge or 3-D charts
  • A legend that relies on colour alone (fails WCAG 1.4.1)
  • Animated count-up numbers (slow to read, noisy for screen readers)
  • More than 4 KPI cards or more than 2 charts above the fold
  • An averaged margin across copper and HMS: it is meaningless

Layout order (see page 7): period control → 4 KPI cards → Needs attention + stock bars → weekly trend + recent activity. Phones: KPI cards 2 × 2, then Needs attention (most useful in the yard), then charts.

Lists

Table, filter and search rules

One DataTable component (TanStack Table v9, server mode) serves every list. The identifier comes first, numbers line up, and the filter state always lives in the URL.

ListColumns, in order
PurchasesDate · Supplier · Material · Tons (t) · Rate (₹/t) · Amount (₹) · Vehicle · Invoice · ⋯
Sales OrdersPO no. · Date · Customer · Material · Ordered (t) · Delivered (t) · Left (t) · Progress · Rate (₹/t) · Status · Deliver · ⋯
DeliveriesDate · PO no. · Customer · Stock from · Material · Tons (t) · Amount (₹) · Vehicle · ⋯
CompaniesName · Type · GSTIN · Phone · City · ⋯
MaterialsName · In yard (t) · Open demand (t) · Buy needed (t) · ⋯

Table rules

  • Rows 40 px on desktop (32 px compact option later), 48 px+ on touch.
  • Sticky header; row hover tint; long names truncate with a tooltip, numbers never truncate.
  • Max 1–2 visible row actions; the rest go in the ⋯ menu.
  • Status = badge with colour and icon and word.
  • Sorting shown with an arrow and aria-sort.
  • No bulk selection in v1: nothing here is a batch job except export.
  • 6–9 columns visible; the rest behind "Columns".

Filters

  • Desktop: one toolbar row, filters apply instantly.
  • Phone: search stays visible; "Filters (2)" opens a bottom drawer with Apply and Reset (batch filtering suits slow data).
  • Sales Orders opens on the Open tab, so completed orders never get in the way.
  • Zero results keep the header and filters visible and offer "Clear filters".

When to validate

React Hook Form: mode: "onSubmit", reValidateMode: "onChange". Limit checks are live warnings (cheap numbers). Research: on-blur validation cut errors 22% (Wroblewski); GOV.UK says never validate while typing: the hybrid respects both.

Good error copy

"Enter tons as a number, like 12.450"
"Choose a supplier"
"Enter the vehicle number like GJ03AX4521"

Avoid

"Invalid input", "Error 422", "Oops! Something went wrong", "Please enter a valid value": no blame, no codes, no "please/sorry".

Why a custom compact formatter?

The browser's built-in compact Indian format printed "1.2KCr" for about ₹119 crore in testing. Money must never pass through JavaScript floats either: API strings go straight to Intl.NumberFormat('en-IN') (which formats strings exactly) and arithmetic like tons × rate uses big.js. Dates go to the API as yyyy-MM-dd, never through toISOString() (which shifts IST dates back a day).

Lead with the answer

Surface exceptions loudly

Micro-interactions and motion

A micro-interaction is a trigger plus its feedback. Motion exists only to give feedback, show a change of state, or show where something came from: subtle, brief, and never in the way of typing.

Keep these six

  • Press: buttons shift 1 px down; no ripple
  • Save: spinner → check for 1.2 s → label
  • Meters: progress and limit fills glide to the new value (300 ms)
  • Copy: icon swaps to a check + "Copied" for 1.5 s
  • Just saved: new/edited row tints green, fades over 1.5 s
  • Overlays: shadcn's default menu, sheet, dialog, accordion, tab motion

Reject these

  • KPI count-up numbers (delay reading the real value)
  • Shaking fields on error (vestibular trigger; never the only signal)
  • Shimmer skeletons (use static or slow pulse)
  • Confetti, parallax, pull-to-refresh, animated illustrations
  • Animation on typing, keyboard focus, or re-sorting large tables

Open questions and next steps

Most of this guide rests on established research and design-system guidance. A few choices are reasoned rather than tested, and a few need small backend additions.

Refresh token in an httpOnly cookieSafer sessions (token never readable by page scripts)Medium

Validate with real users before building everything

  • Run a short session with 3–5 people (owner, accountant, yard staff) on a mid-range Android phone in daylight, using the delivery form and one filtered list.
  • Check: click counts, whether hiding (rather than disabling) buttons for VIEWERs confuses anyone, and whether the trade words ("Left to deliver", "Stock from") read naturally.
  • Confirm the Sunlight theme is readable outdoors and the 44 px targets are comfortable with work gloves.

Still to verify

  • Contrast of every proposed colour pair (tweakcn) in light, dark and Sunlight themes.
  • Vehicle-number and GSTIN patterns against official formats.
  • That the shadcn data-table guide and chosen community table code work with TanStack Table v9.
